### Clean energy and innovation: floating photovoltaics harness unused bodies of water for a more sustainable future.

Floating photovoltaics represent an exciting opportunity, combining innovation and sustainability. This technology, already adopted in countries such as Norway and France, makes use of closed bodies of water, such as lakes and artificial basins, to install solar panels capable of producing clean and renewable energy.

One of the main advantages is the reduction of water evaporation, a crucial aspect during periods of drought. Additionally, the panels, naturally cooled by the water beneath them, operate under optimal conditions, enhancing energy performance compared to land-based installations. Indeed, the efficiency of solar cells decreases as temperatures rise; however, the natural cooling effect of floating panels helps maintain high performance.

**Italy has been one of the pioneers in this technology: as early as 2010, the first floating system was set up in Bubano, between Bologna and Imola. However, the development of these systems has slowed down in recent years, despite the great potential of our territory. Artificial basins, abandoned quarries, and natural bodies of water could host numerous plants, significantly contributing to renewable energy production.**

For example, in Veneto, covering 20% of the surfaces of non-operating quarries would allow for the generation of 3,000 GWh per year, with a positive impact on both the environment and the local economy. Floating PV is a promising solution to accelerate your country’s energy transition, positioning it as a leader in innovative and sustainable solar energy production. For these reasons, Vitruvius & Partners has decided to make floating PV one of its principal business lines.
